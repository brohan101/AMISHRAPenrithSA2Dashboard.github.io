Interpretation

Different measures, deliberate separation

Household income (Panel A) describes the community in which PSHS sits; ICSEA (Panel B) describes the school's student body. ACARA explicitly states that ICSEA is not a measure of family wealth or income. Using two panels avoids implying equivalence.
Catchment caveat

Because PSHS is a selective school, it draws students from well beyond Penrith SA2. The community's income profile cannot be assumed to match the socio-educational background of the school's families. The divergent trends above — stable community income rank yet rising school SEA — are consistent with widening enrolment reach.

Key findings

Rapid LBOTE growth

PSHS LBOTE rose from 23% (2008) to 94% (2025) — a 71 pp increase in 18 years, making it one of the most linguistically diverse selective schools in NSW.

School outpaces community

In 2021 the school was 91% LBOTE while the Penrith SA2 community was 28.1% non-English — a 63 pp gap, up from 49 pp in 2011.

Community diversifying too

Penrith SA2's non-English-at-home share grew from 20.3% (2011) to 28.1% (2021) — a 7.8 pp increase across three Census periods.

Key findings

Income growth tracks NSW

Both Penrith (+47.2%) and NSW (+47.9%) saw near-identical income growth from 2011 to 2021, so the relative gap persists.

Education closing the gap

Penrith's bachelor-degree rate grew by 4.9 pp (2016–2021) vs 4.4 pp for NSW, narrowing the education gap from 8.6 to 8.1 pp.

Absolute gap widens

The weekly income gap grew from $288 to $432 despite similar percentage growth, because NSW starts from a higher base.
